  <section class="space-y-6 max-w-3xl mx-auto">
    <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-neutral-900 dark:text-neutral-100">What does <code>safe</code> mean?</h2>
    <p class="text-neutral-600 dark:text-neutral-400 leading-relaxed">
      Alignment keywords such as <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">center</code> or <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">end</code> can center or push content in a way that overflows the scrollable area on <strong>both</strong> sides. In the worst case you cannot scroll far enough to see where the content starts.
    </p>
    <p class="text-neutral-600 dark:text-neutral-400 leading-relaxed">
      The <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">safe</code> keyword tells the browser: use the requested alignment only if it does not cause that kind of “lost” overflow. If it would, fall back to <strong>start</strong> alignment (the safe edge for reading order) so the content remains fully reachable by scrolling.
    </p>
    <p class="text-neutral-600 dark:text-neutral-400 leading-relaxed">
      Syntax: put <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">safe</code> before the alignment keyword, for example <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">align-items: safe center;</code> or <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">justify-content: safe center;</code>. The opposite is <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">unsafe</code>, which keeps the chosen alignment even when overflow is awkward.
    </p>

    <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-neutral-900 dark:text-neutral-100">Flexbox vs grid</h2>
    <p class="text-neutral-600 dark:text-neutral-400 leading-relaxed">
      The same idea applies to both <strong>flex</strong> and <strong>grid</strong> wherever the Box Alignment properties apply: for example <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">justify-content</code>, <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">align-items</code>, <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">justify-items</code>, <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">place-content</code>, and the <code class="text-purple-600 dark:text-purple-300 bg-purple-50 dark:bg-purple-900/50 px-1.5 py-0.5 rounded">*-self</code> variants on individual items.
    </p>
    <p class="text-neutral-600 dark:text-neutral-400 leading-relaxed">
      You will notice it most in scrollable regions (overflow) or when the viewport is narrower than the aligned group. It is a small addition to your CSS that prevents subtle scroll traps for centered or end-aligned layouts.
    </p>
